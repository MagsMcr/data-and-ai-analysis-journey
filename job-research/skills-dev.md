On boosting credentials without another postgrad
Honest answer: for organisations like Nesta, your portfolio and demonstrated skills will matter more than credentials. But there are specific things worth doing:
Most useful — domain knowledge and network:

Bett Show (January each year, ExCeL London) — the UK's biggest EdTech event. Attend as a visitor. Free or low cost. You will meet everyone who matters in this sector in one place. Given your events background you'll be comfortable working a room here
EdTech Impact testbeds — they run school-based evaluation programmes and sometimes need support
Nesta's own events — they run public lectures and roundtables, many free. Attending and engaging puts you in the room

Volunteering specifically:

National Literacy Trust — already on your target list, they take volunteers for research and data projects
Education Endowment Foundation — similar, research-focused charity, volunteers useful for evaluation work
Doit.life — connects volunteers with IT and data-related projects for nonprofits, specifically useful for gaining hands-on tech experience Prospects.ac.uk
Approaching a small EdTech startup directly and offering to help with data or research in exchange for experience — this works more often than people expect

Credentials that are lightweight but credible:

Google Data Analytics Certificate (Coursera) — recognised, relatively quick, adds a line to your CV
Any short Nesta or Education Endowment Foundation CPD — signals you take their work seriously
Writing about EdTech on Medium (already in your plan from Week 4-5) — positions you as someone with a voice in the sector
